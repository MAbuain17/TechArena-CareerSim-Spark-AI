# Decision strategy

## Career objectives

The policy balances continued play with sustained advancement across career level, wealth, professional skill, relationships, wellbeing and risk control. Higher levels increase published salary, making early sustainable progress relevant to later financial outcomes. Promotion planning considers capability, relationships and delivered work over time.

## Decision procedure

Each decision starts from the current phase, visible state, available choices and relevant feedback. The controller addresses credible immediate threats, then unresolved adverse trends, while preserving opportunities for growth. It compares feasible alternatives by their expected contribution, costs, commitments and exposure to difficult-to-reverse consequences.

When alternatives are close, the controller checks whether its preference remains reasonable under a plausible downside. Effects without sufficient evidence remain uncertain. Decisions use the public handbook and current-run observations; exact promotion requirements and hidden-risk conditions are not assumed.

During quarterly phases, the planner compares development, recovery and mitigation within the remaining budget. It executes one step before revising the allocation. Main actions are selected for their contribution to the current objective and remaining career horizon.

## Feedback and recovery

Immediate observations assess action outcomes. Six-month reviews assess whether the broader strategy is producing sustained progress. A missed promotion prompts a review of the balance between capability, relationships and delivery. Organisational warnings remain active independently of those statistics.

After uncertain execution, the controller reconciles the latest state and logs before another action. It preserves the existing session and resumes from the last confirmed decision point. The official terminal state determines completion.

## Generalisation and operational risks

The procedure uses the current menu and published costs, allowing it to accommodate changes in event wording, ordering and values. Decisions are evaluated from the offered information rather than matched to stored event answers.

The principal risks are context loss, inaccurate attribution of outcomes, failure to act on review feedback and excessive defensive spending. Compact state, targeted log recovery, explicit result verification and comparison of opportunity costs address these risks. Validation must assess execution across complete runs as well as the clarity of the instructions.

## Development findings

The reviewed v3 development run completed 48 months with a quantitative score of 52.33/100. Analysis covered the available replay from month 39 onward. The review identified discrepancies between intended and recorded gains, repeated use of a previously successful policy despite weaker current feedback, and unused Energy at completion.

These findings identify execution priorities for further evaluation: accurate outcome records, follow-through on performance and risk warnings, and explicit assessment of remaining quarterly resources. The local result provides a development baseline; official evaluation averages three runs.
