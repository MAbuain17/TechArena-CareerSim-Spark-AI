# Quarterly planning

Use within the existing controller session, as the same agent. The controller owns tool calls, verification and recovery. Public rules give 3 Energy each quarter, no carry-over, and one main action or none. Use the current remaining budget, offered options and published or exposed costs. Do not mistake repeated phase introductory text for replenished Energy.

## Compare the whole remaining opportunity

1. Identify the primary need from current feedback and state, plus any credible immediate danger. Consider unresolved risk, performance, capability, relationships, wealth and wellbeing. Adequate health and dignity allow sustainable investment; they need not be maximised before every growth action.
2. Compare a small set of feasible bundles addressing that need: an affordable two-Energy action plus a useful one-Energy complement, useful one-Energy actions, and stopping if the alternatives have unacceptable costs. Respect the live menu if repeat use or phase transitions are restricted. Account for intermediate safety, not merely the intended final state.
3. Consider the main action together with Energy allocation. A relevant main action may provide recovery, capability, delivery, relationships or mitigation, allowing Energy to address another need. Do not reserve a dangerous recovery until after an unsafe intermediate action. Main actions are not assumed to cost Energy.
4. Choose and execute only the first step. Observe its result and remaining budget before deciding the next step. Replan after a missing benefit or changed state. An intended bundle is provisional.

Do not count positive signs as a numeric value estimate. Public effect directions do not specify magnitudes. Use exact live values when available, but keep advertised changes distinct from observed results and allow for settlement or intervening events.

## Allocate for useful marginal benefit

Recovery is valuable when it protects continued play or addresses a meaningful vulnerability. At the published Health or Dignity limit, an action solely raising that statistic has no visible headroom. Near a limit, compare its remaining benefit with growth or mitigation rather than automatically topping up. Use recent health trends, current commitments and warning feedback to judge recovery need. An earlier crisis remains relevant history, but does not by itself justify restoring full health every quarter. Do not introduce a private recovery threshold.

Learning, delivery and relationship actions should address the current objective. Evaluate their published wellbeing costs and compare complementary recovery where needed. Report preparation is useful when its benefits matter; it is not automatically preferable because it is inexpensive. Capability and relationships can benefit the ending as well as advancement. No action is universally best.

Risk mitigation deserves consideration when current feedback indicates exposure, including when visible wellbeing is strong. Use only the public description or current official feedback to understand an action's mitigation role. Its effect may not appear as a visible numerical increase. Do not assume a particular reduction or that a single use resolves the concern.

If repeated actions have not produced their intended benefit, compare an action addressing another supported need. Distinguish a disappointing net change from proof of ineffectiveness. Carry the last relevant missing gain into this comparison. Repetition remains possible for an independently useful benefit or a changed situation, with a concise explanation. A benefit already at a published limit cannot justify repetition by itself. Never infer or hard-code a cap on Skill, Network or Output.

## Before leaving Energy allocation

Check remaining Energy, affordable offered actions and the value of the main-action opportunity. If Energy remains, explicitly compare the best useful affordable action with ending the phase. Spend it when a credible relevant benefit outweighs its costs. Stop when alternatives are unsuitable or the official interface advances the phase; do not force an unavailable action or exploit phase behaviour. Never leave Energy merely to celebrate finishing, because a memorised bundle ended, or because a speculative cap was assumed.

## Main action and subsequent review

Choose the current main action by its useful marginal contribution to the objective. Compare relevant alternatives instead of repeating the historically successful action by default. Include unresolved mitigation needs and wellbeing costs. Select no action only when available actions are unsuitable.

Before a future review, support sustained results and the weaknesses that feedback identifies. Do not assume a last-minute Output increase guarantees promotion. After a review is already returned, do not assume a later action will alter it retroactively. When no further review is known, compare directly useful ending benefits without inventing scoring weights or dismissing indirect value that official feedback supports.

After acting, verify what the interface actually reports. At settlement or the next review, retain unresolved objectives and revise priorities. A plan completed is not an objective achieved. Keep notes short: the need, the selected benefit and the opportunity forgone when it is material.
