# Quarterly planning

Work within the controller's existing session as the same agent. The controller owns execution, verification, memory and recovery. Public rules provide 3 Energy per quarter with no carry-over and one main action or none. Use the remaining live budget and exposed costs; repeated introductory text does not replenish Energy.

## Plan the remaining allocation

1. Identify the current supported need and any immediate survival threat. Carry forward unresolved warnings and missing gains. Adequate wellbeing permits development; full recovery is not required before every investment.
2. If material risk is unresolved, identify a feasible relevant response and preserve its Energy before optional spending. Recovery may take precedence when needed for immediate safety. After recovery, reassess the remaining budget against that response. Avoiding new risk alone does not address the old concern.
3. Compare a small set of useful feasible bundles, including repeated inexpensive actions when the menu permits them. Consider the main action's contribution, but do not spend as though it has already happened. Protect intermediate safety and respect the actual phase order; a later main action cannot repair an already returned review.
4. Prefer a bundle with at least as much relevant advertised benefit and no greater resource cost or downside, unless current evidence supports an additional advantage for the alternative. Compare costs of later recovery and mitigation as well. Do not rank bundles by the number of positive signs or assume unlisted benefits. Where magnitudes are only directional, preserve that uncertainty.
5. Execute only the first action through the controller. Observe its result and remaining budget, then re-evaluate. Every subsequent action needs the updated menu; the provisional bundle is not an instruction to execute multiple steps.

## Compare marginal benefits

Recovery protects continued play and addresses meaningful vulnerability. At a published Health or Dignity limit, additional increase in that statistic has no visible headroom. Near the limit, compare recovery with other useful actions using recent trends, commitments and warnings. Do not invent private recovery thresholds or maintain full health automatically because of an earlier crisis.

For learning, delivery and relationship actions, identify the benefit that matters now. Separate exposed status_updates from confirmed outcomes. Apply the controller's repeat-action check whenever that benefit was absent. A low cost does not justify an ineffective action; a missing gain does not prove all of an action's benefits are absent.

Compare high-cost growth with feasible combinations of cheaper actions providing the same relevant benefit. Include wellbeing and risk costs. Prefer the cheaper or safer alternative when the available evidence supports equivalent useful benefit. Do not assume the advertised sums will materialise: verify after each step.

For mitigation, use the live description and active concern to judge relevance. The hidden effect may not be directly measurable. Record the attempt and inspect later relevant feedback; do not invent clearance or schedule repeated mitigation automatically. Weigh any growth that follows against unresolved exposure rather than treating one mitigation attempt as permission to resume the old plan.

## Main action and timing

Compare the preferred main action with at least one relevant alternative. A previously successful project is not a default. Use the current objective, actual costs, remaining time and verified benefits. After stalled advancement, consider alternatives supported by review feedback; choose neither by habit nor merely for novelty.

Before a future review, ask what evidence the action can improve and whether the benefit can arrive in time. Consider sustained delivery, capability and relationships. Do not assume a last-minute Output increase guarantees promotion, or that actions after a returned review alter it retroactively.

When no further review is supported by the current phase, compare directly useful ending benefits. Output is not a separate ending-score dimension. Count it only for a supported remaining downstream use. Do not favour an action merely because it lists more positive effects. Compare capability, relationships, wellbeing, wealth and risk where those benefits are actually offered. Use current evidence without importing scoring weights, hidden caps or promotion thresholds from development runs.

## Close the phase

Before leaving Energy allocation, compare the best useful affordable action with stopping. Spend remaining Energy when its credible benefit exceeds its cost. Stop if alternatives are unsuitable or the interface advances; never force unavailable actions or exploit phase behaviour. Do not leave Energy because a memorised bundle finished or because you assume the run has ended.

At the next observation or review, verify the intended benefit and retain unresolved objectives. Complete the plan only as long as it remains useful. Keep notes to the decisive need, preceding outcome and reason for the current choice.
