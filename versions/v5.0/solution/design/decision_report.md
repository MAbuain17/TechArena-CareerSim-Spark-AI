# Decision approach

The objective is a strong career outcome across 48 months. The agent protects its ability to continue earning and developing, then uses the available opportunities to improve career level, wealth, wellbeing, capability, risk control and relationships. Sustainable advancement matters early because a higher salary can compound over the remaining months.

## Decisions under incomplete information

The policy uses the public handbook, the live menu, current state and feedback from the same session. Promotion requirements and hidden-risk quantities remain unknown. The agent does not estimate an exact distance to an unpublished threshold or carry numerical patterns from a development run into a new run.

The agent asks what most limits progress now, which available response addresses it, and whether the remaining resources can absorb a plausible setback. It compares actual safeguards and obligations rather than ranking options by labels. Both excessive caution and unmanaged ambition can cause failure: delay can damage delivery, while apparent short-term gains can leave lasting obligations or organisational exposure.

Quarterly planning considers Energy and the main action together. Recovery, development and corrective work compete for the same opportunity. The agent preserves intermediate safety, executes one step and revises the plan after observing the result.

## Feedback and follow-through

A review's grade, criticism and advancement outcome are separate evidence. The next objective follows what the feedback supports, without assuming the smallest raw statistic is the promotion bottleneck. Sustained delivery, capability and relationships remain relevant when progress stalls.

Corrective work must address the concern. An improvement in Output does not establish that organisational exposure has fallen. A mitigation attempt is recorded as an attempt, then assessed against later relevant feedback. As the evidence changes, the agent can reduce defensive spending and resume suitable development.

When a promised benefit is absent, the agent compares an alternative before repeating the expense. It allows for delayed settlement and other useful effects rather than inferring a permanent cap. If recent official logs can resolve a material discrepancy, it retrieves a small relevant window and proceeds from that evidence.

## Development evidence

The reviewed v4.2 local run completed 48 months at level five with a quantitative score of 67.28. Its ending report showed room for further improvement in career level, wealth and relationships. The action trace also showed repeated missing gains, a strong preference for one main action and occasions when a plan continued despite feedback that deserved reassessment.

The first official scoreboard listed Spärk AI at 32.33. The organisers stated that the test scenarios differ from the development set and that only the final score is returned. The official component scores and action traces are therefore unavailable. The score gap motivates a broader policy review; it does not identify a particular official failure.

Version 5.0 rebuilds the decision procedure around current needs, relevant remedies and observed outcomes. It reduces repeated instructions and keeps firm constraints on session handling and execution, while leaving contextual trade-offs to the agent. Development examples and synthetic checks remain outside the runtime Skills.

## Evaluation and remaining risks

Structural validation and synthetic decision checks assess loadability, consistency and responses to changed facts. They do not measure performance in the official environment. A complete designated-model run of version 5.0 is still pending.

The principal risks are misreading a scenario, retaining a stale concern, failing to act on a warning, attributing changes incorrectly and losing context during a long session. The Skills address these through faithful comparison, concise recoverable notes, current observations and checks after execution. Their effectiveness still depends on model adherence.

Further development should compare the revised policy with a minimal valid baseline under the same permitted setup. Retain every completed and failed run, assess survival and mean quantitative outcome alongside token use, and avoid selecting a design solely from its best development result.
