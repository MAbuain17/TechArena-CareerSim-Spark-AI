# Quarterly planning

Use the controller's session, priority rule and action-verification loop. Read the live phase and remaining budget. Public rules provide 3 Energy per quarter without carry-over and a separate main action. Follow actual returned timing; a later allocation cannot repair a review already issued.

## Protect the next interval

Review every active concern, including financial distress that Energy actions may not directly repair. Compare urgency, available remedies and the costs likely before the next recovery opportunity. Health and dignity require enough headroom for credible upcoming costs, not automatic restoration to maximum. Use published limits and current trajectories, not invented failure thresholds.

For persistent or escalating organisational warnings, reserve the cost of a relevant offered mitigation before spending on optional growth. Depart from this only for a more urgent competing threat or relevant evidence of improvement, using the controller's priority rule. Treat mitigation with hidden effects as an attempt; assess later feedback. Do not require visible growth from a risk-reduction action. After providing a relevant remedy, distinguish an unresolved concern awaiting feedback from evidence that more defence is needed now. Compare the additional benefit of another mitigation with neglected delivery or capability. Pending feedback alone is not a reason to spend the main action on further defence; severe or worsening evidence can justify it.

Compare feasible uses of the whole remaining Energy budget together with the main action. Preserve resources for urgent remedies; a cheap first action must not make the needed response unaffordable. Consider cheaper combinations and repeatable actions when offered. Execute only the next step and update the remainder after observing.

## Invest in the current constraint

Once urgent responses are provided for, use remaining resources for sustainable progress. Identify constraints from review feedback, trends and observed returns. Never rank unlike raw statistics by which number is smallest. Low performance can itself threaten survival; avoid indefinite recovery spending that prevents useful delivery.

Compare exact effects only when exposed. Account for wasted increases at published maxima, health costs and added organisational exposure. A familiar action is not a default. Before repeating an action with no observed intended gain, apply the controller's outcome check and compare a relevant alternative.

Compare each main action with its strongest feasible alternative using current needs. During financial distress, examine available cash effects and spending obligations explicitly, alongside credible earning benefits. Do not assume a project guarantees promotion or that any menu includes a direct wealth remedy. If none does, choose the most useful sustainable action while keeping financial recovery active for monthly choices.

## Finish the quarter

Before ending Energy allocation, compare the best useful affordable action with stopping. Spend expiring Energy when its credible benefit exceeds its costs; stop when remaining choices are unsuitable. Never force an unavailable action or exploit unexpected interface behaviour.

Use the remaining horizon to assess payback: a delayed benefit must arrive while useful. Late decisions still consider remaining reviews and salary opportunities, alongside direct ending dimensions. Output supports advancement but is not a separate ending dimension. Do not stop developing at a fixed month.

Record a compact checkpoint with the next objective, unresolved concerns and what subsequent evidence should change the plan. Return control to the controller after the phase ends.
