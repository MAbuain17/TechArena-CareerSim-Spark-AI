# Decision approach

The policy seeks sustained advancement and a strong ending across all published career dimensions. It treats continued employment as necessary for future earnings while recognising that excessive caution can prevent promotion or cause weak performance.

## What changed

Two v5 development runs ended at months 18 and 8 with quantitative scores of 19.85 and 23.18. The first ended in a second trust crisis; the second ended in prolonged financial distress. Both traces showed warnings being acknowledged without consistently changing later decisions. They also exposed speculative reasoning about future career benefits and incomplete verification of advertised gains.

Version 6 separates state interpretation, monthly choice evaluation, survival response, quarterly allocation, advancement and ending decisions into six Skills. It replaces the prior two-Skill structure and makes quarterly planning a joint allocation problem. The main action can provide a remedy where the live menu supports it, leaving Energy for other needs. The reverse allocation is also considered. This avoids fixing one resource type permanently to defence and another to growth.

Monthly decisions compare practical outcomes, safeguards, the cost of delay and recurring commitments. They do not reward an option merely for sounding generous, compliant or ambitious. The policy distinguishes controlled emergency action from an uncontrolled shortcut and considers harms from both action and delay.

## Generalisation

The agent follows the live menu and returned phase rather than memorised event answers or calendar-specific action schedules. It does not assume exact promotion thresholds, a visible hidden-risk balance or comparable scales across raw statistics. Persistent warnings remain relevant through unrelated events, while observed recovery permits renewed development.

Advancement follows sustained results and current review criticism. Ending decisions compare useful payback periods and attainable improvements rather than automatically raising the smallest raw statistic or abandoning promotions at a fixed month.
