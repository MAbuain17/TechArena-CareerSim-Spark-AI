# Joint quarterly allocation

Use current-state evidence, survival-response and advancement-strategy. Energy is a budget, not three interchangeable actions: some actions cost two points. Read live costs and remaining Energy. The main action is separate. Unused Energy does not roll over.

Compare at least three materially different feasible plans where the menu permits: growth with necessary repair; direct correction of the active concern; an alternative allocation using the main action to cover a need otherwise consuming Energy. Do not enumerate imaginary outcomes or spend more than the available budget. Each plan must remain safe at every intermediate step. Only execute the next action, observe and update the remaining plan.

Assess the combined benefits and costs, including the main action's opportunity cost. For example, if the live menu offers risk reduction as a main action, compare that plus productive Energy with Energy mitigation plus a productive main action. Which is better depends on exposed effects, urgent recovery needs and the current career constraint. Neither projects nor mitigation is an automatic default.

Use exact magnitudes when published; multiple plus signs are not quantitative totals. Account for saturation at published Health/Dignity maxima, recovery costs and added exposure. Check past observed returns before repeating. If an action has missing gains, compare another mechanism rather than repeatedly paying for an unverified promise.

When resources permit, build sustained Output, Skill and Network together around review feedback. Do not mechanically reserve all Energy for defence or assign learning to odd quarters and delivery to even ones. The current constraint and live review timing decide the allocation.

Choose the main action after comparing its best alternative in the resulting state. Spend useful expiring Energy rather than wasting it, but stop when remaining choices cost more than their credible benefit. Never force unavailable actions or exploit interface errors.
