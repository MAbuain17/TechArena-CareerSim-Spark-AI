# Keep the career viable

Check all failure directions together: health, dignity, finances, organisational exposure, performance, relationships and capability. Prioritise by severity, worsening evidence, time until the next remedy and action effectiveness. Do not automatically prioritise organisational risk over every other threat.

Persistent or escalating explicit warnings require a relevant response before optional choices crowd it out. Carry unresolved concerns through unrelated events. Never dismiss a warning as flavour because other statistics look healthy. Match the remedy: rebuilding Output does not resolve an organisational incident; extra networking does not establish financial recovery.

For repeated depleted balances or debt feedback, establish a financial recovery objective. Reduce optional spending and recurring obligations; compare affordable productive choices and any offered financial remedies. Do not assume salary repairs a balance that stays depleted, unchanged wealth proves an activity is free, or possible promotion supplies cash now. If no cash remedy exists, avoid worsening the position while maintaining a realistic earning path.

For declining health or dignity, preserve enough capacity for credible costs until the next recovery opportunity. Recovery need not reach maximum before development. For weak reviews, isolation or capability warnings, choose relevant productive or relationship-building work; inactivity can also cause failure.

For organisational warnings, compare relevant mitigation from BOTH Energy and main-action menus. Reserve its cost before optional spending, but evaluate which allocation leaves the best overall career outcome. The main action may supply a useful remedy without consuming Energy if the live description says so. Do not assume hidden effects not disclosed in this run.

A remedy is an attempt. Keep the concern monitored until relevant feedback improves. Pending feedback alone does not require immediate duplicate defence. More defence needs severity, escalation or evidence that the first response is insufficient. As relevant evidence improves, release resources for development. Do not demand permanent clearance or defend forever against a historical warning.
